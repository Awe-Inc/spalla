# Sources

What the pedagogy skill is built on, and what each source was used for.
Consulted September 2026.

## Suzuki method

- **Suzuki Association of the Americas — About the Suzuki Method**
  <https://suzukiassociation.org/about/about-the-suzuki-method/>
  The mother-tongue principle, early start, parental involvement, listening.

- **University of Northern Iowa — Principles of Suzuki Talent Education**
  <https://music.uni.edu/suzuki-school/principles-suzuki-talent-education>
  The teaching principles, including review and tonalization.

- **Violinwiki — Sequence of pieces in Suzuki Violin school**
  <https://www.violinwiki.org/wiki/Sequence_of_pieces_in_Suzuki_Violin_school>
  Volume-by-volume contents, books 1 to 10.

- **Violinspiration — Violin Pieces in Suzuki Books**
  <https://violinspiration.com/violin-pieces-suzuki/>
  Complete piece list per volume with the technical focus of each book.

- **Mark Polesky — Suzuki Violin Pieces in their Original Forms, Volume 1**
  <https://www.markpolesky.com/suzuki/vol.1/>
  Provenance and dates for every Book 1 tune. This is the source establishing
  that the melodies are public domain independently of Suzuki's editions:
  "Twinkle" is *Ah! vous dirai-je, maman* (French, 1761), "Lightly Row" is
  *Hänschen klein* (Wiedemann, before 1848), "Go Tell Aunt Rhody" derives from
  Rousseau's *Le Devin du village* (1752), "Minuet 3" is Petzold's BWV Anh. 114.

- **Fiddles, Frogs 'n Horsetails — Twinkle Variations Explained**
  <https://fiddlesfrogsnhorsetails.wordpress.com/bits-n-pieces/twinkle-variations-explained/>
  The mnemonics for the five variations.

- **Violinwiki — Song of the Wind (arr. Suzuki)**
  <https://www.violinwiki.org/wiki/Song_of_the_Wind_(Folk_song)(arrangement_Suzuki)>
  Key, meter and articulation of individual Book 1 pieces.

## Warm-up and technique

- **Johnson String Instrument — Professional Violinist Warm Up Routine**
  <https://www.johnsonstring.com/resources/professional-violinist-warm-up-routine/>
  Duration and structure of a working warm-up.

- **CodaBow — Prelude to Performance: How to Warm Up with Your Violin**
  <https://blog.codabow.com/news/violin-warm-ups/>
  Open-string bow work, long slow strokes, bow parallel to the bridge.

- **Violinist.com — Warm Up Routines** (discussion)
  <https://www.violinist.com/discussion/archive/27749/>
  Schradieck Op. 1 Part 1 and Ševčík as the standard left-hand items;
  metronome at crotchet = 80.

## Scale systems

- **Mark Rush — Scale Up Your Practice: Carl Flesch Distilled**
  <https://www.violinist.com/blog/markrushviolin/20215/28759/>

- **Lessons In Your Home — Scale Systems for the Violin**
  <https://lessonsinyourhome.net/blog/scale-systems-for-the-violin/>
  Flesch versus Galamian; the hand-frame concept; Flesch is double-stop heavy,
  Galamian is rhythm and bowing heavy.

- **Carl Flesch — Scale System** (public-domain scan)
  <https://www.el-atril.com/partituras/Metodos/violin/Flesch_scale_system_for_violin.pdf>

- **Galamian acceleration exercise, applied to Flesch three-octave scales**
  <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fmMw9UjMp7s>
  The 1-2-3-4-6-8 notes-per-bow sequence.

## Intonation

- **Violinspiration — 5 Best Exercises to Perfect Your Violin Intonation**
  <https://violinspiration.com/violin-intonation-exercises/>
  Drone practice, singing before playing, slow practice without vibrato,
  Ševčík Op. 1 and Schradieck for finger patterns.

- **Virtuosic — String Instrument Intonation**
  <https://virtuosic.org/blog/string-instrument-intonation>
  The ringing-tone test in first position, and why the instrument confirming a
  pitch beats a tuner confirming it.

- **Seto Viola — How to practise with a drone**
  <https://www.setoviola.com/post/practicewithadrone>
  Tuning perfect fourths and fifths against a drone; "only one right answer
  where the blend is nice".

- **Violin Lounge — Violin First Position, with finger charts**
  <https://www.violinlounge.com/blog/violin-first-position-explained-with-finger-charts-notes-and-videos>
  Whole/half/whole spacing; the high and low position of each finger.

- **Rozanna's Violins — Half Step and Whole Step Practice for Beginners**
  <https://rozannasviolins.com/blogs/rozannas-blog/half-step-whole-step-practice-for-beginners>
  Finger tapes, and why setting each note against a drone beats them.

## Sight reading

- **The Strad — 10 tips for learning and teaching sightreading**
  <https://www.thestrad.com/playing-hub/10-tips-for-learning-and-teaching-sightreading/8320.article>

- **The Strad — How to help music students with sightreading**
  <https://www.thestrad.com/playing-hub/how-to-help-music-students-with-sightreading/2911.article>
  Percival Hodgson's pattern-recognition system.

- **Violinspiration — Violin Sight Reading: practice methods and exercises**
  <https://violinspiration.com/violin-sight-reading/>
  Read below your level; rhythm before pitch; keep the tempo.

- **The Effects of Technology on the Sight-Reading Achievement of Beginning
  Chorus Students** (ERIC EJ1102254)
  <https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1102254.pdf>
  Summarises Killian & Henry (2005) on practice strategies, and Goolsby (1994)
  on eye movement differences between skilled and unskilled readers.

## Practice science

- **Carter & Grahn (2016), "Optimizing Music Learning: Exploring How Blocked
  and Interleaved Practice Schedules Affect Advanced Performance"**
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4989027/>
  Interleaving beats blocking for retention; immediate fluency during training
  is not a good indicator of long-term learning.

- **The Musician's Way — Varied, Distributed, and Interleaved Music Practice**
  <https://www.musiciansway.com/blog/2017/10/varied-distributed-and-interleaved-practice/>

- **Modacity — Using Spaced Repetition to Achieve Effective Practice**
  <https://www.modacity.co/blog/using-repetition-achieve-effective-practice>

- **Huang et al. (2023), meta-analysis in *Educational Technology Research and
  Development*** — "Gamification enhances student intrinsic motivation,
  perceptions of autonomy and relatedness, but minimal impact on competency"
  <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7>
  The reason stars and streaks are kept small in the UI.

- **JMIR Serious Games (2025), "Identifying Key Principles and Commonalities in
  Digital Serious Game Design Frameworks: Scoping Review"**
  <https://games.jmir.org/2025/1/e54075>
  Cognitive load theory, self-determination theory and feedback as the
  recurring pillars across serious-game design frameworks.

## Free scores

- **OpenScore Lieder Corpus** — 1400+ nineteenth-century songs, CC0 1.0.
  <https://github.com/OpenScore/Lieder> · <https://fourscoreandmore.org/openscore/>
  The source `tools/fetch_library.py` downloads from. CC0 is a public domain
  dedication, so redistribution inside this repository is unambiguous.

- **OpenScore String Quartets** — CC0, but stored as `.mscx` (MuseScore's own
  format), so it needs MuseScore to convert and is not currently used.
  <https://github.com/OpenScore/StringQuartets>

- **IMSLP / Petrucci Music Library** — the largest public-domain score archive,
  but PDF scans rather than machine-readable notation.
  <https://imslp.org/>

- **Mutopia Project** — public domain, but LilyPond, PDF and MIDI only.
  <https://www.mutopiaproject.org/>

- **Wohlfahrt Op. 45 (60 Studies)** and **Kayser Op. 20 (36 Studies)** — the
  standard beginner and intermediate étude progressions, both on IMSLP.
  <https://imslp.org/wiki/60_Studies_for_the_Violin,_Op.45_(Wohlfahrt,_Franz)>
  <https://imslp.org/wiki/36_Violin_Studies,_Op.20_(Kayser,_Heinrich_Ernst)>
